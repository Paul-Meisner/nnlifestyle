# The Army Medic and the Collegiate Athlete Who Built a Practice on Precision

**Genesis Chiropractic · Appleton, Wisconsin**

Alex Sturino enlisted in the Army out of high school in Kenosha and served as a medic with the 82nd Airborne. Medics work fast. You stabilize what's in front of you and you move. What he came away with was a conviction that the fastest intervention isn't always the one that fixes anything — and an interest in what the body does when you help it instead of override it.

Maycee grew up in Freedom, about fifteen minutes from where the practice sits now. She was a two-sport collegiate athlete, took a degree in Exercise Science, and found chiropractic the way a lot of athletes do: it kept her competing.

They met in college, married, and moved to Florida together to attend Palmer College of Chiropractic. Now they run Genesis Chiropractic on South Schaefer Street in Appleton, and they do one thing.

## One technique, on purpose

Most chiropractors are generalists. They're trained in a diversified approach and adjust based on what a patient presents with. It works, and there's nothing wrong with it.

Genesis practices the Gonstead method exclusively, which is a different bet. Gonstead is the most measurement-heavy system in chiropractic — full-spine X-ray analysis, instrumentation that reads temperature differentials along the spine, static and motion palpation, and visualization, all before anything is adjusted. The goal is to identify the single segment that's actually the problem and address that one, rather than adjusting broadly and hoping.

Maycee chose it at Palmer for a specific reason: the amount of data and individuality that goes into each adjustment. That's a preference for precision over volume, and it's a harder way to run a practice. It takes longer. It costs more in equipment and analysis time. It means telling some patients that what they came in for isn't what's wrong.

It's also, from where this site sits, the interesting part. A practice that narrows its technique is making a claim it has to keep proving.

## Families, and the long version of care

Genesis is Webster certified and built around perinatal, pediatric and family wellness alongside general practice. Maycee's stated focus is care for children and pregnant mothers — gentle, specific adjustments rather than force.

That focus has made them a fixture in the Fox Cities' holistic health community. Maycee recorded a four-part series with the Motherhood Alliance, Northeast Wisconsin's directory of holistically-minded practitioners, covering how Gonstead differs from a standard adjustment, what screens are doing to our necks, why families use chiropractic for prevention rather than crisis, and chiropractic through pregnancy and birth.

That's a practitioner who will explain her reasoning at length to people who aren't paying her. It's a reasonable proxy for whether someone believes what they practice.
